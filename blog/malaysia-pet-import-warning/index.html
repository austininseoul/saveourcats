Before you fly your pet to Malaysia — what changed in 2026, and what to get in writing

The rules changed in late July 2026 and were applied to animals already in quarantine. Here is what to check, what to insist on in writing, and where the process can trap you.

We are writing this because it happened to us, and because nobody told us it could.

Our two cats entered Malaysia legally on 16 July 2026. Every document was in order, every fee paid, everything handled through a MAQIS-registered agent. They were given a release date. They did not come home on it, and at the time of writing they are still inside.

What follows is not legal advice. It is the checklist we wish someone had given us. If you are about to fly an animal into Malaysia, read it before you book.

The single most important thing

Get everything in writing, every time, from the start.

Every problem we have had traces back to something that was said rather than written. A release date given verbally. A requirement waived verbally. A payment demanded verbally.

When you are told something — anything — reply by email the same day:

"Further to our conversation today at [time] with [name], my understanding is [X]. Please correct me if that is inaccurate."

That single habit converts a phone call into a record. It costs you nothing. It is the only reason we can prove any of what happened to us.

The rules as they stand

Quarantine is a minimum of 14 days. This was raised from 7 days on 28 December 2024. Plan around 14, not 7 — a lot of older guidance online is still wrong about this.

It can legally be extended to six months. The regulations permit extension based on a case-by-case risk assessment. This is the part almost nobody mentions. "Minimum 14 days" is not a promise of 14 days.

You need an import permit from MAQIS, applied for through a licensed agent registered with MAQIS. As of 2024 all applications are online and must go through such an agent. The permit is valid 30 days from issue, and processing takes roughly 5 to 14 working days.

The rabies antibody test is a pre-shipment requirement. A FAVN test at a WOAH-approved laboratory, result at or above 0.5 IU/ml, from a sample taken not less than 30 days and not more than 24 months before shipment.

Get the report right. It must be in English, on the laboratory's letterhead, and must state the animal's microchip number, the date and place the blood was drawn, the test type, the result, and the signature of whoever issued it. A report missing the microchip number is a report that can be rejected.

Budget properly for the test. Laboratories take 5 to 10 business days to process. With shipping and documents, three to five weeks is realistic. Some routes also carry a three-month waiting period after a successful result — confirm whether yours does before you book flights.

Where the animals actually go

There are four animal quarantine stations. As of August 2026, Batu Maung at Penang International Airport is temporarily closed — MAQIS has published a notice to that effect. If your plan routed through Penang, check its status before you fly.

The others are KLIA in Sepang, Padang Besar on the western Thai border, and Rantau Panjang on the eastern one.

Boarding is charged daily. We are charged MYR 80 per day for two cats. That clock does not stop while a decision is pending, and as far as we can tell it does not stop when the delay is not your fault.

The trap we fell into

In late July 2026 a regulation changed. We do not dispute a country's right to change its rules.

The problem is that it was applied to animals already inside the quarantine station.

Ours were admitted on 16 July. The regulation used to hold them was issued on or about 28 July — twelve days later. There appears to have been no transitional arrangement for animals already in custody. We were told in writing on 2 August that several other agents had animals caught the same way.

So the warning is this: satisfying the rules on the day you fly does not guarantee you will be released under those same rules. We had no way to plan for that, and neither will you. What you can do is keep a complete written record from day one, so that if the terms shift you can show exactly what you were told and when.

If you are asked to pay something

Ask for the paperwork before you pay, not after.

If a fine or compound is imposed, it should come with a written compound notice specifying the offence, issued under the Peraturan-Peraturan Perkhidmatan Kuarantin dan Pemeriksaan Malaysia [Pengkompaunan Kesalahan] 2019 [P.U.(A) 120/2019], and an official receipt.

We paid MYR 2,000 on 7 August 2026 and have received neither.

Note also that MAQIS itself issued fraud warnings in July 2026 about people impersonating its officers to extract payments, and states that legitimate payments go through official systems. If you are asked to pay into any account, ask what it is for, ask for the notice, and ask for the receipt. A legitimate charge survives all three questions.

A checklist before you book

  • Confirm the current quarantine period and station status directly with MAQIS, not from a blog — including this one
  • Titre test done at a WOAH-approved lab, ≥0.5 IU/ml, sample taken 30 days–24 months before travel
  • Test report in English, on letterhead, with the microchip number on it
  • Confirm whether your route carries a post-test waiting period
  • Import permit issued, and note its 30-day validity window against your flight date
  • Ask your agent, in writing, for the expected release date and what could extend it
  • Ask, in writing, what the daily boarding charge is and who bears it if release is delayed
  • Book flexible flights. Pet reservations can usually only be made after ticketing
  • Keep every message, receipt, and certificate. Export your WhatsApp chats with media

Why we wrote this

We are not trying to discourage anyone from bringing their animals to Malaysia. Most imports go through without incident, and the officers we have dealt with have been courteous throughout.

But we walked into this believing that following the rules was enough, and it was not. If this saves one family the month we have had, it was worth writing.

If your animal is caught in the same change, the official complaint channels are SISPAA at the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security, and the Department of Veterinary Services for welfare matters. Separate complaints, properly filed, establish a pattern that a single case cannot.
